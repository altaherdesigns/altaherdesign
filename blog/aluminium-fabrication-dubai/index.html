Aluminium fabrication: Fujairah, Kalba and Dubai

What the trade actually covers, how to judge a workshop, and why the coating decides lifespan more than the metal does.

Home › Blog › Aluminium Fabrication in Fujairah, Kalba and D

Searching for aluminium fabrication near you usually means one of three jobs: a window and door package, an architectural element like a screen or pergola, or a facade and cladding scope. They need different things from a workshop. Al Taher Group fabricates aluminium in-house in the Al Hail Industrial Area, Fujairah, coats it on our own line, and installs across Kalba, Khorfakkan, Dubai and all seven emirates.

The short version

  • Aluminium does not rust, which is why it dominates coastal work in Fujairah and Kalba.
  • The coating decides lifespan, not the metal. Preparation matters more than film thickness.
  • Thermal break is the baseline for any air-conditioned room, not an upgrade.
  • Measure the opening as built, never from the drawing.
  • Ask whether the workshop cuts, fabricates, coats and installs, or only some of those.

What does aluminium fabrication actually cover?

Three broad families. Glazed systems: windows and doors, sliding and folding panels, shopfronts and curtain walling. Architectural elements: pergolas, car shades, louvre screens, privacy panels and balustrade infills. Facade work: composite panel cladding, solid sheet, slatted screens and the sub-frame behind them.

A workshop strong in one is not automatically strong in another. Glazed systems demand hardware knowledge and sealing detail. Architectural work demands finish quality, because the piece is looked at rather than looked through. Facade work demands sub-frame engineering, since most visible cladding faults start behind the panel.

Why does aluminium suit the coast?

It forms its own oxide layer and does not rust. On the east coast, in Fujairah, Kalba and Khorfakkan, salt-laden air attacks steel relentlessly even under a decent coating. Aluminium sidesteps that entirely, which is why it is the sensible default for anything exposed near the sea.

It is also light, which matters more than people expect. A lighter gate leaf puts less load on an automation motor. A lighter pergola needs less foundation. A lighter facade panel is easier to fix safely at height.

How do you judge a workshop?

Four questions, and the answers tell you most of what you need.

What happens in-house? Cutting, fabricating, coating and installing under one roof means one accountable party. Where any of those go outside, ask which and why. We cut on our own CNC machine, fabricate, coat on our own line and install with our own crews.

How do you handle the finish? The single biggest determinant of how aluminium looks in year five. Ask whether coating is in-house, what preparation is done before powder goes on, and how film thickness is checked at edges and drill points. Those are the places a cheap job economises. Our coating line

When do you measure? The right answer is after the opening or structure is built, not from the architect drawing. As-built dimensions drift, and a frame cut to a drawing arrives needing packers to hide the gap.

What is your tolerance on this job? A pergola and a curtain wall are not held to the same standard. A workshop that answers with a number rather than a reassurance is one that thinks about it.

What should the specification say?

For glazed work: thermal break in any air-conditioned room, double glazing with a Low-E coating, and hardware named rather than described as standard. For architectural work: section sizes, span, fixing method and RAL colour. For facade: sub-frame design, bracket type, thermal movement allowance and interface detail where cladding meets glazing.

On thermal break specifically, this is not an upgrade to be talked out of. Bare aluminium conducts heat readily, and an uninsulated frame carries outdoor heat straight into a cooled room. The polyamide barrier inside the profile is what stops it.

What does a proper quote include?

  • System and section: named profile, not just "aluminium"
  • Glazing: unit build-up, Low-E, laminated where safety requires it
  • Finish: RAL colour, gloss level, and whether coating is in-house
  • Hardware: named handles, hinges, locks and closers
  • Site scope: survey, removal of existing, installation, sealing, protection, debris removal
  • Programme: a committed installation date written into the quotation

Frequently asked questions

What does aluminium fabrication include?

Three broad families: glazed systems such as windows, doors, shopfronts and curtain walling; architectural elements such as pergolas, screens, car shades and balustrade infills; and facade work including composite cladding, solid sheet and the sub-frame behind it.

Why is aluminium used so much on the UAE coast?

It forms its own oxide layer and does not rust, which matters in Fujairah, Kalba and Khorfakkan where salt-laden air attacks steel even under a decent coating. It is also lighter, which reduces load on automation motors, foundations and fixings at height.

What decides how long aluminium lasts?

The coating, not the metal. Surface preparation before powder goes on matters more than film thickness, and edges and drill points are where cheap coating economises. Ask whether coating is in-house and how thickness is checked.

Is a thermal break necessary?

For any air-conditioned room, yes. Bare aluminium conducts heat readily and an uninsulated frame carries outdoor heat straight into a cooled room. The polyamide barrier inside the profile is what stops that, and it should be treated as baseline rather than an upgrade.

When should aluminium be measured?

After the opening or structure is built, never from the architect drawing. As-built dimensions drift during construction, and a frame cut to a drawing arrives needing packers and sealant to hide the gap, which is where air and water get in later.

Choosing an Interior Fit Out Company
in Dubai That Delivers

Category A versus Category B, in-house fabrication versus subcontracting, licensing and Civil Defence compliance — the questions that separate contractors who perform from those who only quote.

Home › Blog › Interior Fit Out Company Dubai

An interior fit-out contractor in Dubai is judged on three things: whether they fabricate in-house or subcontract, whether they cover the full scope or a single trade, and whether they can carry the compliance paperwork. Al Taher Group has fabricated and installed fit-out work from its own Fujairah workshop since 1984, across all seven emirates. This guide sets out the questions worth asking any contractor — including us — before a contract is signed.

Key takeaways

  • Fit-out is construction, not decoration — Category B covers everything the developer didn’t.
  • Ask where the joinery, metalwork, glazing and stone are actually made.
  • One contractor across trades removes the interface disputes that cause most delays.
  • Licensing, Civil Defence and Green Building compliance are the villa owner’s exposure, not just the contractor’s.
  • Milestone payments and a written snagging protocol protect you after handover.

What does interior fit-out actually cover in the UAE?

Fit-out is construction; decoration is styling. Decoration deals with soft furnishings, colour and accessories. Fit-out is the physical build of a habitable space — structural finishes, flooring systems, suspended ceilings, joinery, glazing, metalwork, kitchens and built-in systems that become permanent parts of the property.

Category A: what the developer delivers

A villa or apartment handed over in Dubai typically arrives at Category A: screed or raised floors ready for a final finish, ceiling grids, basic MEP distribution, and perimeter cooling connections. Technically functional, far from liveable — bare plaster, unfinished flooring, no kitchen.

Category B: what the owner commissions

Category B is the owner-led phase that turns a shell into a finished home: partitions, marble or tile flooring, bespoke joinery, kitchens, and custom metalwork such as stair railings and entrance gates. Exterior elements including pergolas and aluminium windows fall in the same scope. One difference from Western markets: UAE fit-out contracts routinely bundle fabrication, glazing, marble and ironmongery under a single agreement, which removes a great deal of coordination risk.

Does the contractor fabricate in-house or subcontract?

This is the single most revealing question you can ask: “What percentage of your joinery, metalwork, glazing and stonework is produced in your own workshops?”

It separates manufacturers from project managers who coordinate suppliers. A contractor with its own facility controls quality at source, absorbs design revisions without re-tendering, and bears direct accountability when a component fails on site. Subcontracted fabrication adds a layer of deflection: when a gate arrives wrongly dimensioned or a marble slab is cut to the wrong specification, responsibility shifts to a supplier you have no contract with. On a villa covering gates, railings, kitchens, windows and flooring at once, that risk compounds across every package. Ask to visit the workshop before signing, not after.

Is their portfolio actually relevant to villas?

Ask for three completed villa projects in Dubai of comparable scope and budget, with contactable references. Villa fit-outs are structurally different from office or retail refurbishments — greater ceiling heights, longer bespoke joinery runs, outdoor integration through pergolas and landscaping, and MEP coordination across larger floor plates. A portfolio weighted toward retail may show design capability without villa site experience. When reviewing images, ask about project duration, how many subcontractors were coordinated, and whether the client saw material delays. Those answers tell you more than a photograph.

Are they correctly licensed and classified?

Request the contractor’s Dubai Municipality classification, DED trade licence number, and approved category for villa fit-out works. Working with an unlicensed or wrongly classified contractor creates legal exposure for the owner, not only the contractor — projects can be stopped mid-execution if an inspection finds work outside the approved classification. Contractors registered in free zones may also face restrictions on mainland residential projects. Verification takes minutes and protects months.

Who handles Civil Defence compliance?

Dubai Civil Defence governs fire safety requirements for materials, ceiling systems, insulation and installed fixtures. Non-compliant materials — even where visually identical to approved alternatives — can mean failed inspection, mandatory strip-out and reinstallation at someone’s expense. It can also affect building insurance, which owners rarely consider until a claim is disputed. Any credible contractor can name the person responsible for DCD submissions and estimate approval time within your programme.

Do design and delivery sit in one team?

Fragmented design-and-build arrangements — a designer on one contract, a fit-out contractor on another — are a primary source of scope disputes and cost overruns. When design intent and site execution sit with separate entities holding different commercial interests, the owner becomes the arbitrator of every conflict. Consolidated responsibility removes that dynamic. It is the same logic behind buying metal, glass, stone and joinery from one workshop: our services run under a single project manager rather than across three subcontractor diaries.

What materials can one contractor genuinely handle?

The breadth of material a contractor handles in-house determines how cleanly a project comes together. Where a gate post meets a marble threshold, or a window frame aligns with a tiled sill, tolerances must be coordinated — and no subcontracting chain reliably guarantees that. Scope fragmentation at these interfaces is a common source of remedial cost.

How is the project actually managed?

Ask what platform the team uses, how often clients receive written progress reports, and who the single named contact is. Absence of structured project management is the most common complaint from owners who have had difficult fit-outs — without a reporting cadence, delays accumulate invisibly until they are unrecoverable. A credible contractor answers precisely: platform named, weekly or fortnightly reporting committed contractually, one senior contact accountable from mobilisation to handover. “We communicate daily on WhatsApp” is a meaningful signal about operational maturity.

What happens after handover?

Payments should be milestone-based and tied to verifiable completion events rather than calendar dates — a front-loaded schedule leaves you with little leverage if defects emerge. Warranty practice in Dubai typically runs one to two years on installations, but varies enough that it must be stated in the contract rather than assumed. On snagging, establish whether the contractor commits to a structured walkthrough at practical completion, a written defect register, and a defined rectification timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Category A and Category B fit-out?

Category A is what the developer hands over — base finishes, ceiling grids, basic MEP distribution. Category B is the owner-commissioned phase that makes the space liveable: partitions, flooring, joinery, kitchens, metalwork, windows and outdoor structures. Most villa owners engaging a contractor after handover are commissioning a full Category B package.

How do I tell a fabricator from a contractor who subcontracts?

Ask what percentage of joinery, metalwork, glazing and stonework is made in their own workshops, then ask to visit. A fabricator can show you cutting, welding and finishing; a coordinator shows you a catalogue. Asking where powder coating happens is the fastest single test.

Do I need Dubai Municipality and Civil Defence approvals for a villa fit-out?

Yes, for most Category B work. Municipality classification governs who may carry out the work, and Civil Defence governs fire safety of materials and installed systems. Non-compliance is the owner's exposure as well as the contractor's, and can affect building insurance.

How long does a villa fit-out take?

It depends on scope, but individual elements are predictable: a custom gate is typically 2–3 weeks from design approval, stair railings 1–2 weeks, and a fitted kitchen 3–5 weeks. A full villa package is programmed around those, with committed dates written into the quotation.

Should one contractor cover all trades, or should I appoint specialists?

For a villa package, one contractor is generally the lower-risk route. Separate flooring, glazing, metalwork and kitchen contractors means four timelines, four invoices and four quality standards — and when one runs late, you absorb the delay. A single contract also consolidates liability at the interfaces where trades meet.

Does Al Taher work outside Fujairah?

Yes — all seven emirates, with fabrication in the Fujairah workshop and installation by our own teams. For a same-day emergency callout a local workshop will be faster; for a specified package with a programme, in-house control is the stronger trade.
